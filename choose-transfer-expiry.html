Choose an expiry that fits the handoff

Balance the recipient's schedule with how long the link needs to remain usable.

Start with the recipient's next opportunity

Choose the shortest expiry that gives the recipient a realistic opportunity to download. A colleague waiting in the same meeting needs a different window from a collaborator who will return after the weekend. Tell the recipient the deadline explicitly instead of assuming they will open the link immediately.

Four useful windows

One hour: a live handoff when the recipient is already available.

24 hours: an everyday handoff expected to be collected within a day.

Three days: a recipient working a different schedule or needing a little more time.

Seven days: a planned handoff with a longer collection window; remind the recipient to keep a local copy.

Expiry and revocation do different jobs

Expiry closes the transfer automatically after the selected window. Revocation lets the sender close it earlier through the original browser or, for a transfer uploaded while signed in, the associated account. Use revocation if the recipient confirms receipt and no further downloads are needed, or if the link was sent to the wrong person.

Both affect future access through the service. Neither can recall a downloaded file or prevent someone from forwarding a copy they already saved. Time limits help reduce how long a link remains useful; they are not a substitute for choosing the right recipients.

Plan for missed deadlines

Keep the source files outside RelayDrop. If the recipient misses the deadline, create a new transfer and send the new details. If a deadline is important, verify receipt before it passes instead of relying on the sharing link as your only record.
